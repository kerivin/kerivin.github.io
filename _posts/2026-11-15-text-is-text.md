I like text. It's the most basic form of art among pictures, video, and sound, and is arguably the most minimalistic one. There's not much that can be done with text. Authors naturally use text for its word meanings, but text has more to offer than that. Poetry introduced rhythm, and some use basic formatting such as italics, bold, underlining, etc. That's it, usually.

But every form of art makes me think: what if one used the fact that this is *that* form of art to add something to it? The fact that text has letters. The fact that you read them. The fact that there's a gap between this line and the next one. The fact that there's a language (or languages) involved. The fact that footnotes exist (or are deliberately missing).

I tried to experiment once: I wrote all the speech lines in English and made them rhyme, while writing all the descriptions in my native language. Well, now half of my friends can't read it because they don't speak English, but it was fun. And at the end, it switches: the descriptions become English, and the speech becomes my native language. Idk, I just like how theatrical it feels.

I like playing with it, trying different options besides the story itself. Since one looks at the text, you can make it look good, or ugly, or weird. Since one reads it, you can make it sound good, or ugly, or weird. Since one understands its meaning, you can make it... You get the point. Every way text is perceived can be used for artistic purposes, not just the actual word meanings. I'm curious to see such experiments with text.
